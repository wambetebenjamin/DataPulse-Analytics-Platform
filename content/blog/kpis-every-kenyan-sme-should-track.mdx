Ask a Nairobi business owner how the month went and you will almost always get one number back: total sales. It is the easiest figure to produce and the least useful one to act on. Revenue tells you what already happened. It does not tell you whether it will happen again.

These are the seven measures we install first on nearly every DataPulse account, in the order they tend to change behaviour.

## 1. Gross margin by product, not in aggregate

A single blended margin hides the products that are quietly losing you money. Calculate it per SKU:

> Gross margin % = (selling price − landed cost) ÷ selling price × 100

Landed cost means the cost of the item *plus* transport, duty and spoilage. A retailer we work with in Eastleigh discovered that three of their top ten sellers by volume sat under 4% margin once transport was included. They were busy, not profitable.

## 2. Average order value

Total revenue divided by number of completed orders, over the same window. AOV is the fastest lever most businesses have, because raising it costs nothing in acquisition spend. Track it weekly; if it drifts down while order count holds, your mix is shifting towards cheap items and your margin is about to follow.

## 3. Repeat purchase rate

The share of customers in a period who had also bought from you before. In retail and food service, a repeat rate under 20% means you are renting customers rather than keeping them. The arithmetic is unforgiving: acquiring a new customer costs between five and seven times what it costs to sell again to an existing one.

## 4. Days of stock remaining

Not stock on hand — days of stock. Units in hand divided by average daily units sold over the last 30 days. This is the number that prevents both stock-outs and dead capital sitting on a shelf in Industrial Area.

## 5. Revenue concentration

What percentage of revenue comes from your top five customers or top five products? Above 50% and you do not have a business, you have a dependency. Many Kenyan B2B firms discover this only when one client renegotiates.

## 6. Collection period

For anyone invoicing rather than taking cash or M-Pesa at the point of sale: average days between invoice issued and payment received. Profitable companies close every week in Kenya because the money arrived sixty days after the salaries went out.

## 7. Revenue per staff member

Total revenue divided by headcount. It is crude, and it is the single best early warning that you have hired ahead of demand. Watch the trend, not the absolute value, and compare it only against your own history.

## Making them weekly rather than monthly

A monthly report is a post-mortem. A weekly one is a steering wheel. The practical obstacle is almost never the analysis — it is assembling the data. If your sales sit in M-Pesa statements, your stock sits in a spreadsheet and your staff data sits in a book behind the counter, a weekly cycle is simply too much manual work to sustain, and it collapses by week three.

That is the gap automated dashboards close. Connect the sources once, and these seven numbers recalculate themselves while you sleep.
